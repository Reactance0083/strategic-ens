Questions buyers ask

What do I actually receive?

A time-limited allocation such as acme.productpassport.eth, with the permitted child names, record editing and management actions specified before purchase. You retain your product data and software. You do not receive the root name, control of other branches, permanent rights or an operating product-passport or authorization service.

Do I need to explain my whole project?

No. We ask for your organization and a general use category so the license covers the right naming rights. Confidential plans, source code and trade secrets are not required. If you need unusual permissions or exclusivity, we discuss only the details relevant to that scope.

Why use ENS alongside our existing brand?

An ENS name may add a human-readable on-chain discovery layer for users or systems that already support ENS. It is optional. Your existing product identifiers, data hosting, compliance processes and brand can remain the foundation.

What changes at renewal or when the lease ends?

The agreement must specify renewal pricing or a pricing rule, notice periods, permitted suspension or termination grounds, any cure period, record export, migration and the treatment of existing child names. Annual fees do not imply permanent names or an automatic renewal debit. Do not rely on a yearly naming lease for long-lived product identifiers without an agreed continuity plan.

Does a name verify my company or make my product compliant?

No. A readable name can point to your records in compatible applications. Identity checks, product data, permissions and regulatory compliance remain the responsibility of the systems and organizations providing those services.

Who builds and operates the platform?

The operator, unless a separate services agreement says otherwise. Holding a name does not create product software, compliance capabilities or guaranteed compatibility.

How much does licensing cost?

The proposed annual fees are $2,500 for Company Namespace and $12,500 for Platform License. Enterprise receives a separate proposal. These are published asking prices, not established market valuations. The prepared offers have no contractual identifier-count cap or per-identifier license charge, with an eight-level descendant depth limit. Taxes and separate customer-paid network costs must be disclosed before payment. Enterprise amendments and current private agreements require their own explicit scope.

How do payment and payout work?

PayPal is connected for approved private invoices. Its secure payment page displays the options available to your account and invoice. Payments are received through the licensor’s PayPal business account. This website does not convert PayPal receipts into ETH or send them to an Ethereum wallet. The prepared automatic checkout instead combines native USDC payment and leased-name allocation in one transaction. It is not active yet. A direct transfer to the receiving wallet does not register a leased name. Fees and payment instructions are confirmed in your agreement.

Is a smart contract created when I pay?

Not for every purchase. The prepared managed service uses one controller per activated root and records each customer’s lease in it. The customer approves a combined native-USDC payment and allocation transaction, with a separate exact-amount token approval if needed, and pays its network gas. Actual delivery must be verified. A current private invoice grants only the rights and delivery process in its signed agreement. No automatic purchase service is open yet.

What if a payment fails or my finance team needs changes?

Contact the owner using the invoice reference. A pending bank payment is not a confirmed receipt. Corrected amounts or terms need a new approved invoice, and switching payment routes requires checking or cancelling the earlier request first. Refunds, disputes and delivery remedies follow the signed agreement and applicable provider process.

Can access always be revoked?

ENS rights vary by implementation. Some wrapper permissions and subname rights can become irreversible. A technical control plan must be approved before any permissions are granted; the agreement must match the rights actually available.
