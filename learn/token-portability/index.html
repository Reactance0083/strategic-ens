Practical guide / Published 25 November 2026

A token moved. Did its meaning move with it?

Moving a token is not the same as moving the asset's rights, restrictions, identity, history and controls. Before calling an asset portable, compare what the source system knows with what the target system can preserve and enforce.

Identity Rights Meaning Controls History

01 / The difference

Transfer is one event. Portability is continuity.

On 24 November 2026, the European Central Bank published a proposed Tokenisation Reference Model for standardisation discussions. It treats transferability and portability as different capabilities.

Transferability

Can value move?

A transfer can succeed within one ecosystem when the parties, authorization, compliance and settlement conditions are valid.

Portability

Can context survive the move?

Portability asks whether rights, obligations, metadata, identity attributes and authoritative relationships remain intact across systems.

Operational result

Can the target use it correctly?

A technically visible token may still fail if its legal meaning, servicing history, controls or settlement status cannot be understood or enforced.

02 / Five continuity checks

What must remain understandable?

01

Identity

Is it still the same instrument, issuer and authoritative record?

02

Rights

Do ownership, obligations, restrictions and eligible-holder rules still apply?

03

Meaning

Can both systems interpret the identifiers, fields, events and status in the same way?

04

Controls

Can the authorized party still enforce policy, compliance and lifecycle actions?

05

History

Did servicing, corporate actions, restrictions and audit evidence travel or remain verifiably linked?

03 / Fifteen-minute evidence test

Compare one asset across two systems.

Choose a real design or controlled test. Do not record customer secrets or production credentials in the worksheet.

  1. Name the source system, target system and exact tokenized instrument.
  2. Locate the source evidence for each of the eight worksheet rows.
  3. Locate what the target system receives, interprets and can enforce.
  4. Mark each row matched, partial, missing or not applicable.
  5. Assign an evidence owner and one action for every partial or missing result.

04 / Naming boundary

A persistent name can point to evidence. It cannot preserve the asset by itself.

An ENS name could help software discover an issuer, policy or record reference. It cannot make rights legally portable, align data semantics, preserve settlement finality, enforce controls or prove that two systems interpret the asset consistently.
