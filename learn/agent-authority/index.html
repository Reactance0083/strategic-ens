Two-minute guide / Reviewed 23 November 2026

Before an AI agent moves money, prove four things.

A financial institution does not only need a name for the agent. It needs enough evidence to decide what that agent may do, for whom, under which limits, and with what level of trust.

Identity Authority Mandate Assurance

01 / The decision path

Four records. One controlled action.

On 19 November 2026, the Monetary Authority of Singapore said its next agentic-finance work would develop a common protocol carrying these four categories of information between customer agents and financial institutions.

01

Identity

Which agent is asking, and which person or organization does it represent?

02

Authority

Who granted permission, and is that permission still valid?

03

Mandate

What action, amount, counterparty, time window and conditions are allowed?

04

Assurance

What evidence supports the agent's provenance and reliability?

02 / Practical record

What should the decision record show?

The institution still makes a separate decision for the requested action. A useful record should make the result reviewable after the fact.

Request

Exactly what was asked?

Record the agent, principal, action, asset, amount, destination, time and relevant context.

Decision

Why was it allowed or refused?

Record the policy version, mandate limits, current authority and the specific reason for the result.

Evidence

Can another reviewer verify it?

Keep an independent event record, expiry information and any human approval or escalation.

03 / Important boundary

A readable name can help discovery. It cannot grant permission.

An ENS name could help software locate an agent's policy or identity references. It cannot prove control, establish a legal mandate, enforce a transaction limit, or make the evidence trustworthy. Those checks belong to the authorization and risk systems evaluating the action.
